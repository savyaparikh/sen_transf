Step 1 — Yesterday's close (per CUSIP)
Take CUSIP 93974ERT5 on 2/20. It had 4 ECN_MSG_IDs active that day. Each one has a last offer:

1213057252-A last offer → 2.778
1213091046-A last offer → 2.772
1238449027 last offer → 2.783
1238451182 last offer → 2.780

Average those 4 last offers: (2.778 + 2.772 + 2.783 + 2.780) / 4 = 2.778
That's the baseline for 93974ERT5. Do the same for every other CUSIP.
So now you have:

93974ERT5 (50-100K) → baseline 2.778
594918BZ7 (100-150K) → baseline 3.102
13063DLP4 (<50K) → baseline 2.532


Step 2 — Today a tick comes in
At 9:35 AM on 2/23, a tick hits for 93974ERT5 at 2.770.
bps delta = (2.770 - 2.778) × 100 = -0.82 bps
Same time, 594918BZ7 ticks at 3.091:
bps delta = (3.091 - 3.102) × 100 = -1.12 bps
Same time, 13063DLP4 ticks at 2.521:
bps delta = (2.521 - 2.532) × 100 = -1.12 bps
Notice — each CUSIP is compared to its own baseline. You never mix the 2.778 with the 3.102.

Step 3 — Aggregate across CUSIPs into qty buckets
Those three CUSIPs are in different qty buckets, so they don't get combined. But if you had two CUSIPs both in the 50-100K bucket — say one was -0.82 bps with 3 active dealers and the other was -0.50 bps with 1 dealer — the weighted average would be:
(-0.82 × 3 + -0.50 × 1) / (3 + 1) = -0.74 bps
The CUSIP with more dealers pulls the average more because it's a stronger signal.

That's it. The Avg DoD number in the Excel is just: "how far are today's offers from yesterday's last offers, in bps, averaged across all CUSIPs in that qty bucket, weighted by dealer count."

Before the market opens — run once:
The model looks at yesterday's data and builds a profile for every ECN_MSG_ID that was active. Each profile records: what CUSIP, what ECN (BONDDESK or TMC), what their last offer price was, what time they first posted, and how many times they updated. These are the fingerprints you'll match against today.

A tick comes in today with a new ECN_MSG_ID:
The model needs to figure out: which of yesterday's dealers is this?
It runs the Hungarian algorithm, which tries every possible pairing between today's ECN_MSG_IDs and yesterday's ECN_MSG_IDs for that CUSIP. It scores each pairing mostly on price — if yesterday's dealer closed at 2.778 and today's dealer opens at 2.779, that's a very close match. It also checks if they're on the same ECN. Time of day and update frequency barely matter.
The algorithm picks the set of pairings that minimizes total cost — so it finds the best overall assignment, not just the best match for one dealer.

Each pairing gets a confidence score:
Starts at 100 and gets penalized:

Price gap between yesterday's close and today's open costs 15 points per bps
Different ECN costs 25 points flat
Time gap costs 1 point per hour
Capped at 95 max — you can never be fully certain with anonymous IDs

So a same-ECN dealer whose price is within 0.3 bps scores around 90. A cross-ECN dealer with a 1 bps gap might score 55. You can decide what threshold you trust.

Computing the bps change:
Once a link is made, it's simple. Yesterday dealer X closed at 2.778. Today dealer X (now with a new ECN_MSG_ID) ticks at 2.771. That's:
(2.771 - 2.778) × 100 = -0.70 bps
Every tick from that dealer throughout the day gets compared to their own 2.778 — not a market average.

Aggregating into the chart number:
All matched dealers' bps deltas that fall in the same 5-min bucket and same qty bucket get averaged together, weighted by dealer count per CUSIP. That gives you one line per qty bucket on the chart.

What about unmatched ticks?
If a brand new dealer shows up today that wasn't active yesterday, the Hungarian algorithm can't find a good match (cost too high). That tick gets flagged as unmatched and does not contribute to the chart. This is intentional — you only want to measure how existing dealers moved, not have a new entrant distort the signal.

End of day:
Call reset_intraday(), then pre_market(today_df) — today becomes tomorrow's baseline.
